**Reply within minutes, then follow up on day 1, day 3 and day 7, each time with one useful thing and one clear question. Stop after the fourth message, or as soon as they say no.** Speed matters most: a [Harvard Business Review study](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) found firms that responded to a new enquiry within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to reach the decision maker as firms that waited longer.

## The four-message plan

| When | What to send | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Within 5 minutes** | Thanks, an answer, one question | They're still on their phone and still deciding |
| **Day 1** | The detail they asked for, plus proof | Gives them something to act on |
| **Day 3** | Two clear options (slots, packages) | Makes yes easy |
| **Day 7** | A polite last check, with an easy out | Closes the loop without pressure |

## Example messages

**Within 5 minutes:**
> Hi [name], thanks for asking about [service]! It's [₹ price] and takes [time]. Is this for this week or later?

**Day 1:**
> Hi [name], here's the detail you asked for: [link or 2 lines]. Here's what a customer said last week: "[short review]". Any questions?

**Day 3:**
> Hi [name], we have [Thursday 5pm] or [Saturday 11am] open. Want me to hold one for you?

**Day 7:**
> Hi [name], I won't keep messaging. If you'd like to go ahead later, just reply here and we'll pick it up.

## Five rules that make follow-ups work

1. **One question per message.** "Is this for this week or later?" gets answered. Three questions get ignored.
2. **Use their name, and what they asked for.** It shows a person read the message.
3. **Send at sensible hours.** Roughly 10am to 8pm. A late-night follow-up feels pushy.
4. **Every message adds something.** A price, a slot, a review, a photo. Never just "following up".
5. **Write down where each lead is.** Even a simple sheet beats memory.

## A simple tracking sheet

Make a sheet with these columns, one row per lead:

| Name | Number | Asked about | First reply sent | Last follow-up | Next follow-up | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Status can be just four words: **new**, **talking**, **booked**, **not now**. Check the "Next follow-up" column every morning.

## If you use the WhatsApp Business API

On the API, you can reply freely for 24 hours after a customer's last message. After that, the first message has to be a template approved in advance. So write your day 3 and day 7 messages as templates, and get them approved before you need them.

## Common questions

**How often should I follow up with a lead?** Four messages over about a week works for most small businesses: within minutes, day 1, day 3, day 7.

**Should I call or WhatsApp?** Reply on the channel they used. If they ask a detailed question, offer a call in your WhatsApp reply.

**Can follow-ups be automatic?** Yes. With the WhatsApp Business API and a simple flow, the first reply and the follow-ups go out on their own, and your team steps in when the lead replies.
